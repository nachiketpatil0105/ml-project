The problem, in simple words

You are listening to a playlist. After half of the session, we look at what you did: which songs you skipped and which you kept playing. Then we try to guess which of the next songs you will skip.

This is feature-based prediction. It is not about song titles or artists.

The models never use a song's name, an artist, or a learned "identity" of a song. They use only two kinds of numbers: (1) how the listener behaved earlier in the same session, and (2) numeric properties of the next song, such as energy, tempo, mood (valence) and popularity. The song IDs in the data are used only to look up those numbers and to spot repeats inside a session. Because of this, the model can score a song it has never seen before.

The boosted-tree model scores higher than the best simple rule (95% interval to ). Most of its strength comes from how the listener behaved earlier in the session. Popularity and audio similarity add very little. The Results page gives the details and the Limitations page says what we cannot claim.

MAA (Mean Average Accuracy) is a score between 0 and 1. It checks how often the guesses are right, and early guesses count more than late ones. Higher is better. It is explained step by step on the Evaluation page.

What one row of the data is

One row is one song in the second half of a session. A session has 10 to 20 songs. The first half is what we know. The second half is what we predict. For each song in the second half, the answer is "skipped" or "not skipped" (the field skip_2, the same field the original challenge used).

How the numbers on this site are made

Every number and table on this site is loaded from result files. Each result file is written by one Python script, and each script keeps its results in its own folder: python/01_data_understanding to python/06_explorer. The Reproduction page lists which script made which file. Use the Menu button at the top to jump to any page. Every page also has "previous" and "next" links at the bottom.
