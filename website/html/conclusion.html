Conclusion

Answer to the research question. Yes, classical models can predict second-half skips from the first half of a session, but only to a moderate degree. On test sessions, boosted trees reach an MAA of against for the best simple rule. The gain is (95% interval to ). The next song is guessed right of the time.

What explains the skips. Almost all of the predictive power comes from how the listener behaved earlier in the session ( of what the model learned). Popularity alone is no better than guessing. Acoustic similarity and audio features did not give a gain that we can tell apart from noise. So the part of our proposal that expected acoustic similarity to contribute clearly is not supported. The part that expected classical models to beat simple rules is supported.

About the models. The boosted trees beat the logistic regression by and the random forest by . The three boosting libraries are too close to rank. Good features mattered more than the choice of model.

Feature-based, not song-based. The model uses numbers about behaviour and about the song, not song or artist identity. That is why it can score songs it has never seen.

What next. More data would help the most. After that, models that also see which songs come next, which is outside the scope of this classical-model project.
