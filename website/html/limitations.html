Limitations

What these results do not show

  • Small sample. We have 10,000 sessions and test sessions. The 95% interval of one score is about ± . Differences smaller than this between two models cannot be trusted.
  • The model learns behaviour, not taste. of what the model learned comes from history features. It learns that a session tends to continue the way it started. It does not learn what a person likes. Audio and similarity features changed the development score by only and , which is inside the normal spread between folds (). So this study does not support the idea that audio similarity explains skips.
  • Prediction is not cause. A feature that predicts skips, such as how earlier songs ended, may only be another sign of the same behaviour. Nothing here tells a service what to change to reduce skips.
  • One service, one time. The sessions come from one platform at one time. If a service used our model, it would change what people hear, and so change the data.
  • Fixed prediction point. We predict the whole second half from the middle of the session. A real system could update after every song, which is an easier problem with different scores.
  • No user ID. We cannot separate a person's habits from one session. The results say nothing about single people.
  • Not comparable with the challenge leaderboard. We use the same score and target, but on a 10,000-session sample and our own split. The challenge used 130 million training sessions and a hidden test set. We followed one published description of the challenge for the first-half rule with odd session lengths and did not check it against the organisers' code.
  • No deep learning. We decided to use classical models only. Published neural-network solutions that also see which songs come next scored much higher on the full data. So our numbers are not the limit of what is possible.
  • Search and test use. The settings search is not nested, and the test sessions were scored once with an earlier version of the pipeline before the final run (see the Evaluation page).
  • Re-running. The boosting libraries can give slightly different models on different computers (number of CPU cores, library version). A re-run can change scores in the third decimal place.
  • Early look at the data. We made descriptive plots of the full mini dataset before fixing the split. No model choice was based on them.
